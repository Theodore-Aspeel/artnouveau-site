# Maison-atelier d’Ernest Delune - factual review packet

Date: 2026-09-16
Article id: `maison-ernest-delune-maitre-verrier`
Status: ready for final editorial review, not approved for publication

## Review purpose

This packet resolves the article title's false implication that the building was
commissioned for a master glassmaker. It separates the original commissioner from
the later occupant and records the two construction phases of the through-site.

## Controlling source

- Urban Brussels, Inventaire du patrimoine architectural,
  [Ancien atelier et habitation de Clas Grüner Sterner](https://monument.heritage.brussels/fr/buildings/19656),
  record 19656, consulted 2026-09-16.

The current institutional designation and Rue du Lac address are also corroborated
by a 2025 opinion of the Commission royale des Monuments et des Sites:

  entry dated 2025-09-24.

## Corrected editorial claim

The former title `Une maison d’Ernest Delune pour un maître verrier` was misleading.
The preposition `pour` presented Sterner as the initial client, while the official
record identifies Victor Marchal as commissioner of the 1902 annex and Sterner as a
later long-term occupant.

The title was replaced in all three languages:

- FR: `La maison-atelier d’Ernest Delune à Ixelles`
- EN: `Ernest Delune’s House and Studio in Ixelles`
- NL: `De atelierwoning van Ernest Delune in Elsene`

The stable slug is retained to avoid breaking the public URL. It is a technical key,
not an approved factual formulation.

## Public-copy review

The three introductions now state the safe sequence: the Rue du Lac annex was built
in 1902 by Ernest Delune for Victor Marchal, then occupied for many years by Clas
Grüner Sterner, who kept his workshop there.

The former epigraph referred generically to the `client`, which reinforced the title's
false implication. It now refers to the building's use. The English and Dutch body
copy was also tightened to remove literal, machine-like phrasing while keeping the
same short editorial scope.

The practical date is displayed as `1902 (Rue du Lac)` and `1902 (Meerstraat)` to
avoid suggesting that the 1893 house and 1902 annex form a single construction
campaign.

## Image description

An independent editorial review found that the hero image shows the entrance rather
than the entire house. The FR, EN and NL alternatives now describe the wooden door,
stained glass and circular stone surround visible in the photograph. They do not use
the image itself to infer an architect, date or occupant.

## Claims not approved from this packet

Do not publish as established facts:

- that Clas Grüner Sterner commissioned the building;
- that the building was originally constructed for a master glassmaker;
- that Ernest Delune certainly designed the 1893 Rue de la Vallée house;
- that Sterner and Delune collaborated regularly. The official inventory calls this
  probable only;
- that the entire through-site dates from 1902.
